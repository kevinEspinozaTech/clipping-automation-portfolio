## Context

Before committing further resources to European geography as the channel's definitive focus, Stage 2B researched 8 candidate faceless-YouTube niche families using real public sources, scored them on 10 criteria (demand, monetization feasibility, production cost, automation capability, visual-rights availability, long-form/Shorts fit, competition, 12-month sustainability, Europe/international fit, and originality feasibility), and ranked them. Full per-criterion evidence: [`../../templates/niche-scoring-matrix.csv`](../../templates/niche-scoring-matrix.csv). Pivoted comparison: [`../../templates/niche-format-comparison.csv`](../../templates/niche-format-comparison.csv).

## Provisional shortlist (top 4 by computed total_score)

| Rank | Niche | Total score | 
|---|---|---|
| 1 | European geography, borders & curiosities | 7.4 |
| 2 | Nature & wildlife facts | 7.3 |
| 3 | Everyday economics & curious data | 7.1 |
| 4 | Cities, transport & urbanism | 7.0 |

The next four candidates (Infrastructure 6.8, History 6.3, Science & technology 6.2, Kids/family 5.8) were researched with the same rigor but did not make the shortlist — see the full report for their profiles.

### 1. European geography, borders & curiosities

**Advantages:** Highest computed score; strong demonstrated demand (RealLifeLore, Half as Interesting); strong visual-rights availability already proven in this project's own Stage 2 work; a genuine Europe-first, Spanish-language differentiation gap (incumbents are English/global-oriented); near-inexhaustible topic supply.

**Disadvantages:** The broad-topic space is already dominated by large incumbents (7.9-9M subscriber channel exists); requires ongoing custom map/data-visualization production, which is a real skill/tool investment.

**Risks:** Standing out against RealLifeLore-scale channels on broad topics; must keep differentiating via the Europe-specific/Spanish-language angle rather than competing head-on globally.

### 2. Nature & wildlife facts

**Advantages:** Second-highest score; explicitly documented as low-cost and low-competition by independent industry sources; strong long-form and Shorts fit; durable, evergreen demand ("curiosity about wildlife never diminishes").

**Disadvantages:** Weaker natural fit with a "European geography/infrastructure" brand identity if that identity is kept; would represent a bigger pivot than the other three finalists.

**Risks:** The documented low cost of this niche is partly explained by reliance on licensed/stock wildlife footage — a genuinely faceless, rights-clean version of this niche requires the same disciplined per-asset visual-rights verification as this project already applies elsewhere ([`../03-visual-asset-rights-policy.md`](../03-visual-asset-rights-policy.md)), not an automatic pass just because "stock footage is abundant."

### 3. Everyday economics & curious data

**Advantages:** Highest monetization-feasibility score of all 8 niches (finance is the top-CPM category per industry sources); strong, already-proven synergy with this project's Stage 2 research (Eurostat data is real, open, and already used); good Europe fit via EU statistical sources.

**Disadvantages:** Moderately saturated (Economics Explained, Two Cents already established); requires careful, source-disciplined scripting given the real-money stakes of financial content.

**Risks:** Financial/economic content carries elevated scrutiny (accuracy expectations, potential regulatory sensitivity around anything resembling financial advice) beyond typical geography/nature content.

### 4. Cities, transport & urbanism

**Advantages:** Highest Europe/international fit score of all 8 niches — the flagship example in this space (Not Just Bikes) is already built around a European city; moderate competition (fewer large incumbents than history or science); strong long-term topic supply (thousands of cities, ongoing policy debates).

**Disadvantages:** Lower raw demand signal than geography or history in this research pass; the most-cited example (Not Just Bikes) is personally-narrated, not faceless, so a faceless approach in this niche is less proven than for geography.

**Risks:** Urban-planning content can be perceived as more opinionated/advocacy-adjacent than pure geography facts, which may affect brand-safety/monetization perception (not independently confirmed either way in this pass).

## Recommendation (non-binding)

Based purely on the computed scores and the evidence gathered, **European geography, borders & curiosities remains the strongest-scoring candidate**, and it earned that position on the same footing as the other 7 niches, not by assumption — its main advantages are compounding: proven demand, proven visual-rights availability (already demonstrated in this project's own prior work), and a real, evidenced differentiation gap (Europe-first, Spanish-language) rather than a purely re-hashed global-English concept. **Everyday economics & curious data** is a strong secondary candidate specifically because of its superior monetization profile and direct synergy with the geodata/statistics research skills already built in Stage 2 — a combined or sequential approach (geography-and-infrastructure now, economics as a closely related second content pillar later) is worth human consideration.

**This is a recommendation, not a decision.** The final choice, including whether to combine niches, narrow further, or select a candidate not in this shortlist, is left to human judgment.
